## 3. Claims versus evidence, and method issues

1. The central deliverable is still missing. The title and contribution (i) promise certification of a given plan. The paper reports the capacity at which each hub reaches gamma (buffers 35-76% over Q), but never the overflow probability of the plan at its own capacity Q, nor the fraction of routes or hubs that pass at each gamma. In the route experiments c is set so that p is about gamma, not at the planned Q. A reader cannot tell whether the plans pass or fail. If a 35% buffer is needed at 1e-3, then every hub fails at Q; say that and give P_hub(Q) and P_route(Q). This was round-1 item 9 and is untouched. It is one more hub run per level.
2. The "exact conditional program" is a discretised, extrapolated, truncated-domain reference, not exact (the summary file itself says so). Its own error estimate is above 1% for 68% of the 60 route-level rows (median 1.3%, maximum 8.5%) and is 2.8-9.1% of p for the six exact hubs. Yet SMIS agrees within 0.1-0.2% and the paper times the exact program as if it were a 1% method ("t(0.01) = 297 s"). The abstract and Section 1 call it "exact". Either (a) call it a quadrature reference and give its actual accuracy, or (b) time it at the grid that gives 1% true accuracy. The median |p_hi - p_lo| is 0.2%, so a coarser grid (fewer than 41^2 nodes, Gauss-Hermite instead of trapezoid) probably reaches 1% at a fraction of the cost. The "hundred times" claim depends on this. For the hubs the exact check cannot detect bias below its own 3-9% error estimate, so "settles this" (Section 7.5) is too strong.
3. Confounding is not broken. In the 120 routes, all 40 balanced routes (varrho > 0.75) are Northern Vietnam, where pickup = delivery was assumed. In the 30-route rival set, 15 of 15 Vietnamese routes are the varrho > 0.75 group and all 15 Guangdong routes are not. So every statement about "balanced routes" for GL, conditional MC and single shift (Table 3 lower block) is also a statement about Vietnam. The pickup-halving test uses 13 Guangdong routes at gamma = 1e-4 only, and only 2 of them show a material single-shift bias. The abstract sentence "Halving the pickups ... produces the same failure, so it follows the end-mode structure, not the region" is stronger than 2 of 13 routes. Fix: rescale the pickups of the 15 Vietnamese routes the other way (e.g. x2 so varrho falls) and show the failure disappears; and halve pickups for more Guangdong routes at 1e-5 and 1e-6.
4. Diagnostic (Figure 3, m_hat). It uses only the 30 sensitivity routes at gamma = 1e-4. Of 240 rows, 11 have m_hat >= 1e-3, and for m_hat < 0.01 the error (0.2-0.5%) is at or below two standard errors (the dotted line), so it cannot separate "of the order of m_hat" from noise. Only three points (m_hat 0.0136, 0.0199, 0.135) carry the claim. The 120-route set, where the bias is 3-6% median and 40% q90, has no m_hat in the paper. Compute m_hat for all 480 route-gamma rows and plot that. Also the threshold 0.75 is "empirical" on one region.
5. Timing fairness. (a) The wall times were measured in sharded runs on a 4-core machine (baselines run 270.7 min wall for 315.7 min of reference time, so jobs overlapped); the paper says "one core" but not whether other jobs were running. State the load, repeat a subsample of the timing runs alone, and report the interquartile range. (b) The exact program, SMIS and the rivals are different implementations; say what language and whether the DP is vectorised. (c) SMIS is run at N = 1e6 and the rivals at 2e4 simulator calls, 2,000 inner programs, 5x2,000 subset draws; the error columns then mix method and budget (the text admits this). The claim that GL "inherits the single-mode bias" is inferred from a median error of 7-16% against a sampling error of 2.5%; run GL and conditional MC at 1e6 draws on the 15 balanced routes and report the signed error to show bias directly. (d) The route-day sample ratio "1,800x" is almost entirely the CE pilot (2.8-3.9e6 draws); in wall time it is about 13x (43-55 s vs 3-4 s, r2_routes.csv). State the wall-time ratio next to the 1,800x. (e) The rival set (30 routes, 10 with L >= 9, maximum L = 13) says nothing about longer routes, which is where a classical DP loses.
6. Coverage study. Only 10 Guangdong routes (8 with DP reference, 2 with CE reference), only gamma = 1e-4, N = 1e4, proposal built once per route (so build variability is excluded), no balanced routes, nothing at 1e-5 or 1e-6 where weights are heavier-tailed. Pooled coverage 94.4% is 1.4 binomial SE below 95%. Note also that in Section 7.2 only 87-90% of the DP deviations lie within 1.96 reported SE; the paper explains it by reference error, which then conflicts with the 1.2% error estimate. "Certificate" should stay in quotes until coverage is shown at the levels and on the routes where the claim is made.
7. Hub bias. The crude-MC check shows mean z = +0.59 over 20 hubs (14 of 20 positive, t = 2.3, mean relative difference +1.5%, median +1.8%) and the exact check shows 5 of 6 positive. An importance-sampling estimator with an exact likelihood ratio is unbiased, so a positive difference is not explained by missed modes (which would bias low). Candidates: the DP reference drops routes (the table shows "routes (reachable)" of 3 of 5 for BACKAN, 6 of 7 for several hubs, while Section 7.5 says "no route was unreachable"), grid truncation, or the calibration step selecting c on the same SMIS run (winner's-curse type selection: c is chosen where the estimate hits gamma, and the 1e5-draw final estimate is a fresh run, so this is less likely). Resolve the "reachable" definition; it affects the exact hub numbers directly. "Conservative for certification" is right only if the bias is upward in P.
8. Hub validation range. Hubs reach 597 customers and 100 routes; the largest checked against crude MC has 102 customers, against the exact program 55. There is no hub check at 1e-5. The calibrated buffers at 1e-5 therefore rest on SMIS alone, with no independent check at the level that matters.
9. Route-day. The load/time split "evenly" is by design, and the paper says so (good). The simulator has no time windows (lateness against a nominal arrival plus slack), yet Section 1 says "time windows". The only crude check is gamma = 1e-3; at 1e-4 and 1e-5 MDIS is compared with a CE mixture built on the same simulator and quantile transform. A shared bias is invisible. Run crude MC at 1e-4 on a small subset (1e9 draws on 5 routes is feasible with vectorised code) or use an independent implementation.
10. Theory status. Theorem 1 is labelled a sketch. Theorem 2 has a proved part (a) (its proof is a short argument that relies on Theorem 1) and a sketched part (b); the "sharp criterion" wording is removed from the contributions but contribution (iii) still reads "the asymptotic threshold (varrho > 1/4) holds for every route", which is a computation, not a theorem. Lemma A2 is proved for kappa >= 3 but stated for kappa >= 4; harmless but tidy it. The statement of Theorem 3 ("one block dominates the other by a factor of 4 in every retained stage") is not checked on the plans (only kappa is).
11. "Efficient". The efficiency claims are variance per draw, RV about 4-6, equal to CE (3.8-6.0) and slightly worse than the single shift in easy bands (3.76 vs 3.51). The gain is the saved pilot (3-4x at eps = 1%). The abstract says "at least two orders of magnitude faster" against the DP and conditional MC; against CE (the real practitioner default) the gain is 1.5-2x in wall time at eps = 0.01 (Table 3: 2.6 vs 4.6 s; 3.3 vs 6.4 s) and the single shift is as fast and accurate except on balanced routes. The paper says this honestly in Section 7.2 but the abstract does not.
12. Reference to a "release". The Code and data paragraph says everything "is released with the paper" but gives no repository, DOI or commit. IJOC needs a persistent link at submission.

## 5. Verdict and the changes that matter most

Verdict: major revision. The theory is honest now and the numbers are mostly right. What IJOC readers will ask is whether the plans pass, whether the reference and the timing are fair, and whether the balanced-route finding is about routes or about Vietnam.

Changes, in priority order:

1. Report the plan-level result. For all 66 hubs and for the 120 (or all 6,242) routes give P(overflow) at the planned Q at each gamma, and the share that passes. Add this to Table 6 and the abstract. Without it the title's claim is not delivered.
2. Fix the reference and the timing comparison. Rename it "quadrature reference"; report its true accuracy against a finer grid; time it at the grid that reaches 1% true accuracy; report the implementation and machine load; repeat key timings on an idle machine. Rewrite "at least two orders of magnitude" to the measured 90-115x (or the new ratio).
3. Break the region confound with an experiment, not a sentence. Rescale the pickups of the 15 (or all 40) Vietnamese routes so that varrho falls below 0.5 and show the single-shift bias disappears; halve Guangdong pickups on more routes at 1e-5 and 1e-6. Change the abstract's "same failure" to what the 13-route data show (2 material cases).
4. Test the m_hat diagnostic on the 120 routes and 480 rows, where the bias is large, and correct the "0.5-1.6" and "noise level when m_hat < 1e-3" statements (section 2, item 4).
5. Resolve the upward hub difference: define "reachable" in the exact hub program, explain 3-of-5 and 6-of-7 against "no route was unreachable", run the exact or crude check at gamma = 1e-5 and on a hub with more than 100 customers, and test the sign (t = 2.3 on 20 hubs).
6. Make rival comparisons budget-fair: run GL two-step and conditional MC at 1e6 draws on the 15 balanced routes and report signed errors; add a smaller-pilot CE and a "two end shifts, no optimisation" estimator so that the pilot saving is separated from the mixture. State wall-time ratios next to the sample-count ratios (route-day 13x, not only 1,800x).
7. Extend coverage: 1e-5 and 1e-6, routes with varrho > 0.75, proposal rebuilt per replication, and ESS. Keep the word "certificate" only if it holds there; otherwise "estimate with confidence interval".
8. Correct the seven numbers in section 2 (items 1-7) and the three wording issues (halving pickups, abstract medians, attribution definition). Add n by reference kind to Table 2.
9. Shorten the abstract to under 200 words, with the validation level for each claim. Fix Figure 1 (Guangdong delivery block) and colour Figure 2 by region.
10. Give a persistent code and data link (repository, commit or DOI) and describe the CE baseline and the 30-route selection rule. Add the missing Gaussian-maxima references after verifying them.

\begin{letter}{The Editors\\Methodology and Computing in Applied Probability}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

I submit the manuscript ``Two end modes: logarithmic asymptotics and importance
sampling for the peak load of a pickup-and-delivery route'' for consideration in
\emph{Methodology and Computing in Applied Probability}.

The paper studies the tail of the peak of a load process that arises in vehicle routing:
a maximum of sums of gamma variables coupled by one-factor Gaussian copulas. It proves
logarithmic asymptotics with explicit spectral scales, a sharp threshold for the
efficiency of single-shift importance sampling, an exact description of how the single
shift underestimates without inflating its own reported variance, and a sufficient
condition, reduced to a computable covering constant, for the logarithmic efficiency of
a mixture of shifts. A short numerical section checks the conditions and predictions on
routes of two regional plans.

In the interest of transparency: an earlier, computation-oriented version of this work
was submitted to the \emph{INFORMS Journal on Computing} (JOC-2026-10-OA-2667). The area
editor, Prof.\ Shane Henderson, declined it without review because the model is narrow,
noted that the analytical treatment of the importance-sampling problem was its valuable
part, and recommended a journal devoted to probability. The present manuscript has been
rewritten around that analysis, which combines rare-event asymptotics with the design and
efficiency of simulation estimators and so falls within the scope of this journal: all proofs are
complete (the earlier version gave sketches), the results on the silent underestimation
and on mixture efficiency are new, and the computational material has been reduced to an
illustration. I intend to publish under the journal's standard (non-open-access)
model.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Code and data are publicly
available. There are no competing interests and no funding to declare.

\closing{Sincerely,}

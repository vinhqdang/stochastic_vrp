# Novelty check: "fleet-pooled Bayesian recourse" (searched 2026-11-27)

Method: web searches (extended + standard) on arXiv, INFORMS, ScienceDirect, RePEc, Polytechnique Montreal repository; the key paper (Florio et al. 2023) was downloaded and read in full text (pdftotext). Search tool is US-only and snippet based; Google Scholar and Crossref could not be queried directly, so DOIs marked (unverified) come from memory/reference lists, not from a resolver.

## Bottom line

- Point (1) online Bayesian learning of a day-level multiplicative demand factor during route execution, feeding the recourse (restocking) decision: **ALREADY PUBLISHED** (Florio, Gendreau, Hartl, Minner, Vidal, EJOR 2023). Their model is almost exactly the proposed one: a single unknown "external factor" chi acting multiplicatively on all customer demands (E[xi_i | chi] = mu_i chi), a Gamma prior, Poisson-gamma conjugate update after each revealed demand, posterior predictive demand for the next customer, an optimal-restocking DP that uses it, a convergence theorem, and a comparison against the independence-assuming optimal restocking policy ("cost of independence", savings above 10%). Do NOT claim (1) as novel.
- Point (2) pooling across vehicles: **apparently OPEN for Bayesian learning, and explicitly flagged as open by Florio et al.** Florio's learning is per route ("Throughout this section, we assume a fixed route"), with a single vehicle's observations. Their Section 7.2 names "inter-route recourse / pooling recourse policies" as unsolved and says "there is currently no measure of the benefits" of cooperative recourse relative to optimal restocking. Cooperative recourse exists (Ak & Erera 2007; Zhu et al. 2014) but it is customer-sharing/reallocation between paired vehicles, not posterior pooling of a latent factor. I found no paper that pools observations of a shared latent day factor across vehicles. Caveat: absence of evidence from snippet-based search; a Scholar forward-citation check on Florio et al. 2023 should be done before submission.
- Point (3) comparison against plug-in and re-optimisation triggers: **PARTIALLY covered.** Florio et al. compare Bayesian-aware restocking against the independent (non-learning) policy, which is a weak form of plug-in baseline. A comparison to an explicit certainty-equivalent / plug-in estimator of the factor, and to re-optimisation triggers, together with a fleet-pooled version, is not in that paper. Single-vehicle rollout/reoptimisation (Secomandi 2001; Secomandi & Margot 2009; Goodson et al. 2013/2016) never learns the distribution.

Defensible positioning: "Florio et al. (2023) learn a shared demand factor along one route and list inter-route pooling as open. We show/quantify the value of pooling the factor posterior across a fleet (the fleet sees N-times the evidence, earlier), in a setting with SPD, three-class fleet costs, and handoff recourse, and compare against per-route learning (Florio-style), plug-in, and trigger rules." The headline contribution must be the *marginal value of pooling over per-vehicle Bayesian learning*, with Florio-style per-route learning as the main baseline, not "we introduce Bayesian learning in VRPSD". Also note the effect of pooling may be small or analytically obvious (more observations => sharper posterior); a theorem or sharp quantification (e.g. posterior variance/regret scaling with number of routes and stops observed before a given stop) would strengthen it.

## Closest papers

### 1. Florio, Gendreau, Hartl, Minner, Vidal (2023): the near-scoop for (1)
Recent advances in vehicle routing with stochastic demands: Bayesian learning for correlated demands and elementary branch-price-and-cut. European Journal of Operational Research 306(3):1081-1093. DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2022.11.040 (taken from the arXiv bibliography; the ScienceDirect PII is S0377221722008566). arXiv:2302.02538.
What it does: introduces an unknown "external factor" (weather, trends, events), uniform multiplicative effect on all customers' demand rates, prior on the factor, Bayesian update after every revealed demand, posterior predictive demand for the next customer depends on the cumulative observed demand; extends Yee-Golden optimal restocking to this posterior; proves the learning procedure converges to the true factor; simulations on 20 routes per size (3 to 15 customers) with Poisson demands rate mu_i * chi, savings over independence-assuming restocking exceed 10%. Also an E-BP&C for VRPSD.
Closeness: (1) very high (same factor model, same update, same use in recourse); (2) none implemented, learning is per fixed route, and Sec. 7.2 lists inter-route/pooling recourse as open; (3) partial (compares to the independent-demand optimal policy only). Differences from the proposal: delivery-only, restocking-only actions, conjugate Poisson-gamma closed-form, one route at a time, no fleet, no plug-in estimator, no re-optimisation triggers, no SPD, no handoff.

### 2. Florio, Feillet, Poggi, Vidal (2022/2023): partial reoptimisation
Vehicle routing with stochastic demands and partial reoptimization. Transportation Science (related DOI listed on arXiv: 10.1287/trsc.2022.1129; arXiv:2201.08866). Studies the "switch policy" (swap of successive customers) with optimal restocking, BCP for a priori routes. Conclusion: only marginal gains; suggests reassigning customers among vehicles jointly may be needed. Closeness: (1) none; (2) none (single route recourse); (3) a re-optimisation-style comparison, no learning. Useful as the benchmark for what reoptimisation triggers buy.

### 3. Ak & Erera (2007): paired recourse
A paired-vehicle recourse strategy for the vehicle-routing problem with stochastic demands. Transportation Science 41(2):222-237. DOI 10.1287/trsc.1060.0180. Vehicles are paired; one is a stable-route vehicle, the other's recourse depends on its own and the partner's unserved customers; tabu search; 3% to 25% savings on 50+ customer instances. Closeness: (1) none, demand distributions known; (2) cooperation yes, information pooling for learning no (partners share customers, not beliefs); (3) no.

### 4. Zhu, Rousseau, Rei, Li (2014): paired cooperative reoptimisation
Paired cooperative reoptimization strategy for the vehicle routing problem with stochastic demands. Computers & Operations Research 50:1-13. DOI 10.1016/j.cor.2014.03.027 (from the repository record; CIRRELT-2013-73 is the working-paper version). Single pair of vehicles with partial reoptimisation and communication, multi-vehicle case by clustering customers into pairs. Closeness: (1) none, known distributions; (2) communication between two vehicles, but of realised loads/positions for re-sequencing, not of a statistical factor; (3) no. Florio et al. cite this and Ak & Erera as the existing cooperative-recourse work and say its benefit versus optimal restocking is unmeasured.

### 5. Popovic (1995): old Bayesian VRP
Vehicle routing in the case of uncertain demand: a Bayesian approach. Transportation Planning and Technology 19(1):19-29. (DOI not retrieved; publisher page returned 403; author recorded only as "J. Popovic".) Demand distributions at nodes are unknown and treated with a Bayesian approach. I could not read it; from the abstract it appears to be Bayesian updating of demand distribution parameters for a stochastic routing problem, with no recourse-at-stop policy and no fleet pooling. Should be read before claiming "first Bayesian VRPSD" in any form. Closeness: (1) partial historical precedent for the idea of Bayesian demand learning in routing; (2) none known; (3) none.

### 6. Secomandi (2001); Secomandi & Margot (2009); Goodson, Ohlmann, Thomas (2013, 2016): reoptimisation/rollout baselines
- Secomandi, N. (2001). A rollout policy for the vehicle routing problem with stochastic demands. Operations Research 49(5):796-802. DOI 10.1287/opre.49.5.796.10608. Single vehicle, rollout, known distributions.
- Secomandi, N., Margot, F. (2009). Reoptimization approaches for the vehicle-routing problem with stochastic demands. Operations Research 57(1):214-230. (DOI not verified.)
- Goodson, J.C., Ohlmann, J.W., Thomas, B.W. (2013). Rollout policies for dynamic solutions to the multivehicle routing problem with stochastic demand and duration limits. Operations Research 61(1):138-154. DOI 10.1287/opre.1120.1127. Multi-vehicle (fleet state in the rollout) but known distributions, no learning.
- Goodson, J.C., Thomas, B.W., Ohlmann, J.W. (2016). Restocking-based rollout policies for the VRPSD with duration limits. Transportation Science 50(2):591-607. DOI 10.1287/trsc.2015.0591.
Closeness: (1) none; (2) Goodson 2013 is the only fleet-aware dynamic policy, still without learning; (3) these are the natural "re-optimisation" comparators.

Not read in detail; (1) to (3) no.
- Inventory analogues: Scarf (1959) and Lariviere & Porteus (1999, Management Science 45(3):346-363, stalking information: Bayesian inventory management with unobserved lost sales), Braden & Freimer (1991) conjugate newsvendor families, Petruzzi & Dada (2002), Bisi & Dada (2007). Citing them supports the claim that the update is textbook, which also means the update itself is not a contribution.
- Bayes-adaptive POMDP view: any unknown-parameter MDP augmented with a posterior is a Bayes-adaptive MDP; the QMDP-style posterior average of per-theta value functions is a standard approximation in the POMDP literature (Littman, Cassandra, Kaelbling 1995, from memory, not searched here). Using it is an implementation choice, not a novelty; also, QMDP ignores the value of information, which should be stated as a limitation.
- Online learning in routing under other uncertainty (Thompson sampling for travel times / energy, e.g. Lagos et al. 2023 online shortest path problem; Chalmers EV navigation): different uncertainty, no capacity recourse.

## Risks for the proposed paper

1. Reviewer will point to Florio et al. 2023 immediately; the paper must name it as the per-route special case and win on pooling, not on learning.
2. If all routes in a fleet run in parallel, the pooling benefit depends on timing (how many stops are observed before a given decision) and on how large the factor variance is relative to idiosyncratic variance; with a conjugate Poisson-gamma or Gaussian model the gain is closed-form and may look incremental. A result stating when pooling matters (many short routes, high factor variance, late-route decisions) with a regret or value-of-information bound would be defensible.
3. QMDP-averaging over a policy library ignores information-gathering value; compare against full Bayes-adaptive DP on small instances or justify.
4. Popovic (1995) is unread; Secomandi-Margot, Rostami et al. DOIs unverified. Add to VERIFY_CITATIONS.md before use.
5. Should-do before claiming: Scholar "cited by" on Florio 2023 and Ak & Erera 2007 (2023-2026) for any pooling follow-up; search ride-hailing / dynamic fleet-management literature for "shared latent demand factor across vehicles" (not done exhaustively).

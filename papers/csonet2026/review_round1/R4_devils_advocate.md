No formal claim I tested is false. The theorems are correct. The defects are in significance, in the modelling premise, and in how the case study and the interpretive claims are framed.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The paper concedes in its first page that MWHED is 1||Σ w_j U_j, and that hardness, the pseudo-polynomial DP and the FPTAS are textbook. What remains as the "transportation contribution" is a premise and three corollaries. The premise is that every visit is a self-contained depot round trip, so a dispatch is a job on one machine. That premise is what turns routing into scheduling, and it is false for exactly the instance the paper uses to ground itself. On the Camp Fire geometry the depot spokes are 26 to 37 km long, while Concow, Paradise and Yankee Hill lie within about 10 km of each other. A crew that drives depot to Concow and on to Paradise reaches Paradise in under 30 minutes at 80 km/h. The model charges Concow's 39-minute round trip first and arrives at minute 61. Re-solving with a path model and the paper's own speeds and hazard times serves all four communities, 100% of the weight, against the paper's 68-70%. The case-study "lesson" (a Concow detour makes Paradise late, so protect the dominant site first) is produced by the premise, not by the fire.

The three "added results" are not substantive. The classification is an 8-case corollary the authors call "elementary". The m-vehicle result is the transversal matroid with m slots per position, and it extends verbatim to vehicles of different speeds, which the paper lists as open. The heuristic "unbounded ratio" results use two-site instances against rules nobody proposes, while the paper's own experiments show greedy repair at 0.99+. The "tight" FPTAS bound is tightness of an analysis for a variant that a one-line completion step repairs completely.

Finally, the paper's own sensitivity analysis shows input uncertainty moves outcomes by orders of magnitude more than any approximation error the FPTAS introduces. The only part that would matter, the stochastic problem, is treated as future work.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations / Paths
1. **A path/TSP-with-deadlines model for clustered sites** (orienteering with time windows, or TSP with deadlines) explains the Camp Fire differences without any "brittleness" story, and is the standard transportation model for a single crew visiting sites in sequence. The paper never compares its spoke model against even a trivial path model on its own coordinates (C1).
2. **The dominant-weight explanation**: the case-study results are fully explained by one site holding 68% of the weight and having the latest deadline. No theory in the paper is needed to reach "Paradise first" (M3).
3. **Magnitude, not heterogeneity, as the driver of hardness**: weak NP-hardness is a statement about numeric encoding. A parameterization by max(p_i) or by the number of distinct values would explain the tractable cases more faithfully than "which vector is constant" (M2).
4. **A transversal-matroid / bipartite-matching presentation** subsumes Thm 5 and its natural extensions (vehicle speeds, machine-dependent equal sizes) in one paragraph, replacing the one-theorem-per-case structure (C2, M4c).
5. **Robust/chance-constrained or two-stage formulations** are discussed but not solved, although the paper's own sensitivity study shows they are where the decision value lies (M4b).

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Emergency operations and fire-agency planners: no practitioner validation that depot spokes, population weights and a zero-service-time crew model match any dispatch practice (the real Camp Fire response centred on evacuation, not crews arriving).
- Residents of small communities (Concow, Yankee Hill) who are never dispatched to in the optimum except as add-ons: the utilitarian population-weight criterion and its equity effects are not discussed.
- Hazard modellers whose forecasts the paper treats as exogenous: their uncertainty bands, and the suppression-spread feedback, are outside the model.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes that making the ordering decision exact is the bottleneck of the dispatch problem. Its own sensitivity study (nominal optimum fully on time in 20.7% of draws; a one-line rule within 1.4% of the drawn optimum) says the bottleneck is model and input error, not combinatorial optimization.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The revision is unusually honest about what is classical (abstract, Sec. 1, Sec. 2, the summary table, Remark 2). The self-qualification is a strength and removes any plagiarism-of-results charge; the objection is that, once removed, little remains.
- All proofs I checked (Lemma 1, Thms 2, 3, 4, 5, Props. 6-8) are correct, and Thm 5's union-find correctness argument (Step 5) is careful.
- The decision to separate real inputs from disclosed assumptions in Sec. 5.6, and to report the return reading alongside the arrival reading, is good practice; it is the reason C1 and M1 could be found at all.
- The "active scaling" experiment design (Sec. 5.3) is a sensible response to the vacuous-rounding problem in the accuracy table.
- The matroid counter-example in Related Work (sizes 2,1,5 with D=5) is correct and illustrates the exchange failure clearly.
